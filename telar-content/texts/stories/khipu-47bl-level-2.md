A khipu found in Cutusuma suggests that khipus potentially held more information than only numbers. Further information can potentially be found in ply direction and cord placement. Ply direction can take two forms: S ply or Z ply. 
The Cutusuma khipu recorded livestock.  The final twist of the cord representing females had a S ply while the male cord had a Z ply. 
It is hypothesized that ply direction aligns with the “Theory of Markedness”. The “Theory of Markedness” states that less common states are more rare because they are deemed less valuable (marked) while more common states are ubiquitous because they are deemed valuable (unmarked). S ply is more common while Z ply is more rare. Female sheep, who can bear young and increase herd size, are deemed more valuable than males. A similar hierarchy is also seen in cord placement, with male cords placed above lambs but below ewes (adult female). 
It is possible that khipu ply direction and cord placement can tell us more about valued possessions in Inca society alongside numbers. ![A Drawing of the Cutusuma khipu; S ply and Z ply](https://nimidosanjh.github.io/test/iiif/objects/un/un.jpg)
