---
title: "Significance of Different Colors "
---

It was recently discovered that different names of tributaries in Inka culture might have been coded into Khipu lines. Specifically in the Santa Valley Khipus, there were thirty different colors which may have corresponded with the census which recorded around thirty names in the region. Additionally, one source suggested banded strands indicated Moiety divisions; a dual organization system within the Inka culture that is determined from birth. An individual is designated a certain group (Hanan or Hurin), which corresponds to different responsibilities such as political duties or what region a group would live in. Another study showed that the Khipu colors may have also indicated different tasks within Ayullus. Within the moiety groups, there are many different ethnic groups, called Ayullus which have certain tasks to carry out. It was found that there were six different tasks assigned to an individual, indicated by color but also by the different knots tied at the ends.
