---
title: "More than Knots"
---

For many years, scholars believed that khipus were simply accounting tools used to record numbers. Today, however, researchers are asking much bigger questions. Could khipus have stored names, histories, social relationships, or even stories? If so, they may represent one of the most sophisticated systems of communication developed in the ancient Americas.

The khipu challenges modern assumptions about writing and knowledge. Rather than using ink and paper, the Inca relied on cords, knots, colors, and other visual features to preserve information. This website explores how khipus were made, what they were used for, how colors contributed to their meaning, and who created and interpreted them. By examining these questions, we can better understand how the Inca organized knowledge across an empire without a conventional alphabetic writing system.
