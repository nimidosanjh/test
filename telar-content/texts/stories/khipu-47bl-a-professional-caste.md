title: "A Professional Caste Within the State Hierarchy"
---

These khipukamayuq formed a professional caste embedded in the Inka administrative hierarchy. The office was typically hereditary, passed from father to son, and its holders were stationed at every level of the empire's bureaucracy, from local communities up through provincial and imperial centers. There they recorded census figures, labor obligations, tribute, storehouse inventories, and herd counts, while some elite specialists also maintained historical and genealogical khipus. The position carried real trust and prestige: Spanish chroniclers described khipukamayuq as accountable to the state, reporting penalties for inaccurate records. They were not the highest nobility but skilled servants of it, and their literacy in the medium set them apart from ordinary subjects.
